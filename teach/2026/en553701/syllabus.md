# Course description

This course is designed to prepare students for the Real Analysis part of the introductory exam of the Department of Applied Mathematics and Statistics. In this course we will cover fundamental topics in real analysis, such as, Set Theory, Metric Spaces, Continuous Mappings, Uniform Convergence, Differentiable Mappings, Inverse & Implicit Function Theorems, and Integration Theory. The syllabus of the exam can be found [here](https://engineering.jhu.edu/ams/introductory-exam-syllabus/). Past exams can be found [here](https://engineering.jhu.edu/ams/past-exams/).

## Textbook
The textbook for the course is *Elementary Classical Analysis*, by J. E. Marsden and M. J. Hoffman (2nd edition). This book will be helpful to you as a reference and also as a source of practice problems.

You can collaborate with your classmates in small study groups of up to 3 members. If you do so, please mark at the beginning of each assignment the full name of your collaborators. Each member still needs to submit their own solution. Homework submission will be in person. They need to be handwritten on specified blue books. I will collect homework at the beginning of the lecture. 

Two late submissions can be accepted up to 48 hours without penalty. After 48 hours, submissions will not be accepted.

## Using Generative Artificial Intelligence

You can use generative artificial intelligence tools (ChatGPT, New Bing, Gemini, etc.) to help you study. Find the learning strategy that best suits you. However, you have to complete your homework by yourself. This means you can never copy any answers from Chatbot or Copilot directly to your homework. You should learn, understand, internalize, and create your own solution. Rule of thumb: when you actually write down the solution to your homework, you should be able to answer an entire question with only your pen and pencil, without looking at a screen, as if this were in an exam.

For good and bad use of AI, see the attached departmental guideline. It contains useful prompts for AI-assisted learning. For our class in particular, I will suggest first doing things in the old-fashioned way, studying with your classmates, and making use of the office hours.
